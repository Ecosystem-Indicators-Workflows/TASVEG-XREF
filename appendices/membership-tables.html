15  Membership tables

The following diagram illustrates a minimal example consisting of two classifications with two concepts each, and lines joining the source and target concepts with membership values.

These relationships can be represented in different tabular formats:

15.1 Membership matrix

A membership matrix will have rows for the source classificaton and columns for the target classification:

Concept Z Concept W
Concept A 100% 0%
Concept B 40% 60%

The size of the matrix will increase with the number of concepts in each classification (n rows for the source and m columns for the target).

These matrices will expose the information transparently, and can be useful for data entry purposes, but they can become very clunky when the number of concepts is both classifications is large, and are not optimal to store multiple interpretations (see next section).

15.2 Long-format table

An alternative representation of this information is a membership table in “long” format:

Source Classification Target Classification Membership
Concept A Concept Z 100%
Concept B Concept W 60%
Concept B Concept Z 40%

The size of the table will increase with the number of links between classifications (\(n \times m\) rows, but only three columns). If we ignore or filter out the zero membership values, then the size can be reduced considerably.

15.3 Wide-format table

A membership table can also be represented in wide format if we can assume that each source concept matches maximum of q target concepts:

Source Concept Target Concept 1 Membership Target Concept 2 Membership
Concept A Concept Z 100%
Concept B Concept W 60% Concept Z 40%

This table will have \(n\) rows for all source concept, but a fixed numner of columns (\(1 + (q \times 2)\))

15.4 Multiple interpreters

Tables in long and wide formats can incorportate multiple interpreters by adding columns to identify the interpreter.

For maximum transparency, these tables could also include columns for date of interpretation and for documentation of methods. This would allow better filtering and comparison of multiple interpretations.

An example with a long format table:

Source Target Membership Interpreter Date Methods
Concept A Concept Z 100% Interpreter A 25 May 2011 Match descriptions
Concept B Concept W 60% Interpreter A 25 May 2011 Match descriptions
Concept B Concept Z 40% Interpreter A 25 May 2011 Match descriptions
Concept A Concept Z 80% Interpreter B 25 May 2025 Compare attributes
Concept A Concept W 20% Interpreter B 25 May 2025 Compare attributes
Concept B Concept W 80% Interpreter B 25 May 2025 Compare attributes
Concept B Concept Z 20% Interpreter B 25 May 2025 Compare attributes
Concept A Concept Z 100% Interpreter A 25 May 2026 Revised opinion
Concept B Concept W 60% Interpreter A 25 May 2026 Revised opinion
Concept B Concept Z 40% Interpreter A 25 May 2026 Revised opinion
